1. Welcome to Our Planet

Dear Tiny Terra Families,

Welcome to our little planet! We started Tiny Terra Academy with one big dream: to create a school where every child feels like the most important explorer in the world. A place where learning smells like fresh soil, sounds like laughter, and looks like a child fully absorbed in play.

We draw from the best of Japanese early-childhood wisdom — the quiet discipline of ikigai, the gentle persistence of ganbatte, the beauty of seasonal awareness — and blend it with India's rich tradition of storytelling, community, and warmth. The result is a school that is both globally inspired and rooted in our local culture.

Thank you for trusting us with your child's earliest years. We do not take that lightly.

With gratitude and green thumbs,
The Tiny Terra Team

2. Educational Philosophy & Cultural Pillars

Tiny Terra does not follow a single rigid curriculum. Instead, we curate the best elements from proven global frameworks and weave them into a cohesive, joyful daily experience:

Montessori Principles

Child-led learning, hands-on materials, mixed-age respect, and a prepared environment that invites independence. Children choose their work and develop intrinsic motivation.

Reggio Emilia Approach

The classroom is the "third teacher." Children are capable, creative researchers. Documentation of learning (art, projects, portfolios) makes thinking visible. Strong emphasis on the arts and collaborative inquiry.

Forest School / Nature-Based Learning

Regular outdoor time in natural settings. Risk-positive play, seasonal observation, gardening, and sensory nature experiences build resilience and environmental stewardship.

We call this blend our "Seed of Potential Framework" — every child is a seed with unique gifts; our job is to provide the right soil, light, and water.

From Japan

  • Ikigai — Finding purpose and joy in small, meaningful acts
  • Ganbatte — Gentle, persistent effort; never giving up
  • Wabi-sabi — Appreciating imperfection and the beauty of natural things
  • Seasonal Awareness (Shizen) — Celebrating the rhythms of nature through art, food, and story
  • Hansei — Age-appropriate self-reflection and emotional regulation

From India

  • Storytelling (Katha) — Rich oral tradition, folk tales, and moral narratives
  • Community & Seva — Belonging, sharing, and caring for others
  • Celebration of Festivals — Cultural identity through joyful rituals
  • Sanskrit Values — Ahimsa (non-violence), Satya (truth), Seva (service)
  • Yoga & Pranayama — Age-appropriate movement and breathing for focus

Our curriculum addresses all five domains outlined in the NEP 2020 Early Childhood guidelines:

  • Physical Development: Gross/fine motor play, yoga, outdoor exploration
  • Socio-Emotional Development: Emotion coaching, conflict resolution, empathy circles
  • Cognitive Development: STEM provocations, problem-solving, inquiry projects
  • Language & Literacy: Bilingual (English + Hindi/regional), phonics, storytelling, library time
  • Creative & Aesthetic: Art, music, drama, nature crafts, clay, and loose parts
Assessment at Tiny Terra is observation-based, not test-based. Teachers maintain portfolios and share learning stories with families each term.

4. Health, Safety & Nutrition

The health of every child and staff member is our top priority. To protect the whole community:

  • Children must be fever-free for 24 hours (without medication) before returning to school.
  • Children with vomiting or diarrhea must stay home for 48 hours after the last episode.
  • If your child shows symptoms at school, you will be called immediately and must pick up within 45 minutes.
  • Contagious conditions (chickenpox, conjunctivitis, hand-foot-mouth, etc.) require a doctor's clearance letter to return.
We know it's hard to keep a mildly sick child home. Please trust that this policy protects your child too — a classroom of 12 little ones can spread illness very quickly!

First Aid & Medical Emergencies

All teaching staff are trained in Pediatric First Aid. A first-aid kit is maintained on-site. In the event of a serious injury or medical emergency, we will call emergency services first, then contact parents immediately. The nearest hospital and emergency numbers are posted in every classroom.

Tiny Terra Academy is a nut-aware school. We take allergies very seriously.

  • Disclose all known allergies and dietary restrictions on the enrollment form.
  • Children with severe allergies will have an individual action plan kept on file and shared with all staff.
  • If your child requires an EpiPen or other emergency medication, please provide a labeled supply to the class teacher at the start of each term.
  • Our meal plan menus are designed to be nut-free, low in processed sugar, and accommodate common dietary needs (vegetarian, Jain, gluten-sensitive, lactose-sensitive).
Update us immediately if your child develops a new allergy or intolerance during the year. Do not wait for the annual enrollment renewal.

Physical Safety

  • All play equipment is age-appropriate, inspected regularly, and maintained.
  • Outdoor play areas are fully enclosed and supervised at all times.
  • Socket covers, rounded furniture edges, non-toxic materials, and locked storage for cleaning products are standard across all rooms.
  • Our staff-to-child ratios exceed the minimum regulatory requirements.

Access Control

  • The school gate is locked during school hours. All visitors must buzz in and sign the visitor register.
  • Only authorized pick-up persons listed in your enrollment form may collect your child. No exceptions.
  • If custody arrangements change, please inform us in writing immediately.

Child Protection Policy

All staff at Tiny Terra Academy have undergone background verification. We maintain a strict child safeguarding policy. Any concern about a child's wellbeing — inside or outside school — will be handled with the utmost care, confidentiality, and in compliance with applicable child protection guidelines. Parents are encouraged to speak to the Principal if they have any concerns.

We believe food is culture, connection, and nourishment. Our meal program is designed around the Japanese concept of ichiju sansai (one soup, three sides) adapted for Indian palates:

  • Whole grains, fresh vegetables, and seasonal produce are the foundation
  • Dal, sabji, roti/rice, and a seasonal fruit or vegetable every day
  • No processed foods, no artificial colours, no refined sugar in school meals
  • Children are never forced to eat; we offer, encourage, and make it enjoyable
  • Monthly menus are shared in advance via the school app
Children who bring lunch from home: please keep it warm in a thermal tiffin, nut-free, and avoid chips, candy, or sugary drinks. We eat together as a community and model mindful eating every day.

Parent Notice Board

A physical notice board outside each classroom door is updated weekly with learning themes, upcoming events, and any important notices. Take a moment to check it at drop-off!

  • Term Reports: A narrative progress report is shared at the end of each of the 3 terms. Reports focus on development, not grades.
  • Scheduled PTMs: Twice a year — at the end of Term 1 and Term 2. Slots are 20 minutes per family.
  • As-Needed Conversations: If we observe something important, we will proactively reach out. Likewise, please do not hesitate to request a meeting anytime.
  • Daily Handovers: At drop-off and pick-up, teachers share brief highlights or any notes about your child's day.
We ask that formal concerns or feedback be raised with the class teacher first. If unresolved, escalate to the school coordinator, and then the Principal. We value open, respectful dialogue.

At Tiny Terra, parents are not just observers — you are part of the village that raises the child. We invite you to:

  • Talent Sharing Sessions: Come in to share a skill, story, or cultural tradition with the class (cooking, craft, music, storytelling).
  • Garden Days: Monthly parent volunteer slots for our school garden.
  • Festival Celebrations: Join us for Diwali, Holi, Japanese New Year, and our seasonal Harvest Festival.
  • Annual Day & Showcase: A joyful celebration of the year's learning — not a performance competition!
  • Parent Circles: Quarterly informal parent gatherings to share experiences, discuss child development topics, and connect as a community.
Children whose parents are visibly engaged with school feel more secure and enthusiastic about learning. Even small gestures — reading the newsletter, waving hello to the teacher — make a big difference.

At Tiny Terra, we do not use punitive discipline. Our approach is based on positive guidance — helping children understand the impact of their behaviour, develop emotional vocabulary, and make better choices.

  • We use redirection, co-regulation, and natural consequences — never time-outs, shaming, or physical correction.
  • Children who need extra support are observed closely; patterns are discussed with parents collaboratively.
  • We model the behaviour we want to see: calm, kind, clear, and consistent.
  • Biting, hitting, or persistent aggressive behaviour will be addressed immediately with the child, documented, and discussed with parents on the same day.
Consistency between home and school helps enormously. If you are navigating a challenging behaviour phase, please talk to us — we can share strategies and work together as a team.
